Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. Across Laredo, Laredo Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

Here is what the work covers: standing sewage is pumped out, contaminated water is extracted from the structure, porous materials the sewage touched (drywall, insulation, carpet and padding) are torn out and disposed of properly, hard surfaces are disinfected thoroughly, antimicrobials are applied, and the structure is dried so mold does not follow. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

Signs It Is Time for Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Dark, dirty water or sewage rising out of a tub, toilet or floor drain
  • Sewage residue visible around drain openings or on basement floors
  • Toilets or drains that gurgle whenever you run water somewhere else
  • Family members falling ill, especially with gastrointestinal trouble, for no obvious reason
  • Soggy, smelly ground over the sewer line or leakage at the cleanout cap
  • Carpet or flooring contaminated when a toilet overflowed with solid waste
  • Several fixtures backing up at once instead of only one

What a Professional Brings to Sewage Cleanup

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Every 2 to 3 years, get a sewer camera run through the line to spot developing issues
  • Keep cooking oil, grease and food scraps out of the drain, where they build into clogs
  • Install a backwater valve on your sewer connection to prevent backups from the municipal system
  • Use drain strainers in showers and sinks to catch hair and debris
  • In an emergency a plumber needs the cleanout, so learn where yours is and keep it in good shape
  • Wipes clog sewer lines even when the label says flushable, so only toilet paper goes down the toilet
