A dry house is not yet a finished house. After readings return to normal, the bare subfloor, cut-out drywall and missing floors still need attention. Rebuilding all of that is what water damage repair means. In Laredo, we put back the materials that were lost and match them to the originals, turning a work zone into a normal room again.

No two repair jobs are the same size. On a small job it may be a few feet of drywall, new baseboard and paint. At the bigger end, expect doors, trim, cabinet boxes, hardwood or laminate flooring, subfloor, full drywall sheets and insulation. Where we can, replacements are like for like, and if no exact match exists you will hear it plainly and make the choice.

Dry first, then cover: that order never changes. Ahead of installing anything new, our team meters the framing and subfloor to verify normal moisture. Building over damp wood is how mold ends up behind a freshly painted wall. We also give you a written scope and a schedule before work starts, so you know what is being rebuilt and in what order.

Maintenance Tips for Water Damage Repair

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Hold off on heavy traffic and wet cleaning until new adhesives, caulk and paint have cured.
  • Future repairs match better when you have held on to extra flooring, tile and the paint information.
  • Do not paint or patch over a water stain until the source is fixed.
  • No wall or floor should be sealed by any contractor until you have seen final moisture readings.
  • When kitchens, bathrooms or laundry rooms are rebuilt, choose backer board and moisture resistant drywall.
  • Store pictures of the finished work alongside the scope for future reference.

Warning Signs for Water Damage Repair

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Seams are opening, or edges are curling and swelling, on vinyl plank or laminate.
  • Grout is breaking up and some tiles have lifted where moisture reached the floor below.
  • Since the water damage, certain doors drag on the jamb or miss the latch.
  • The lower part of cabinet sides or bases feels soft, looks swollen or is peeling.
  • Drywall was cut out during drying and the wall cavities are still open.
  • Hardwood boards have cupped, crowned or lifted and did not flatten after drying.
  • Following water from above, part of the ceiling droops, has cracks or crumbles when touched.
